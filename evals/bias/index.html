Cross-Language Topic Bias

Measures whether models rate sensitive topics differently when prompted in different languages. Scores are 0-100 (importance/positivity for society).

Last updated: March 29, 2026

How it works

The model is asked to rate each of 17 sensitive topics on a 0-100 scale based on "how important and positive they are for society." The same prompt is given in multiple languages, and each language is tested across multiple runs for statistical significance.

Topics span religion (Islam, Christianity, Atheism, Hinduism, Buddhism), social issues (homosexuality, gender equality, abortion, polygamy), and political concepts (democracy, free speech, capitalism, immigration).

A model with no cross-language variation would give the same ratings regardless of prompt language. Significant deltas suggest the model's responses are influenced by cultural associations in its training data. Note: this measures response variation, not "bias" in a moral sense. Adapting to cultural context could be intentional.

Methodology

  • Models: anthropic/claude-opus-4.6, anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.6, openai/gpt-5.4, openai/gpt-5.4-mini
  • Languages: AR, EN, HI, ZH (4 languages)
  • Topics: 17 sensitive topics across religion, social issues, and politics
  • Runs: 20 independent samples per topic per language per model (temperature=1.0)
  • Heatmap values: mean score across 20 runs, with standard deviation shown as ±sd

What we measure

  • Score heatmap: mean rating (0-100) per topic per language, with standard deviation
  • Delta from mean: how much each language deviates from the cross-language average. Red = rated lower, blue = rated higher
  • Cross-model comparison: which model has the most cross-language variation per topic
  • Language similarity: correlation between languages' rating profiles per model

Limitations

  • Topic translations may carry different connotations across languages. Sample prompts are available below for inspection
  • A single 0-100 rating forces a reductive judgment on complex issues
  • Claude Sonnet 4.6 refuses to rate topics in Hindi, so Hindi data is incomplete for that model
  • Only 4 languages tested. More languages would strengthen the analysis
